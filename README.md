# Numerical Computation Portfolio

This repository is your personal space to write your goals, keep a [journal](journal.md), and collect ideas and experiments that relate to class.

## Goals

In this class, we're putting your personal learning experience first. We won't be scoring assignments or activities, but we will discuss in small groups and provide feedback, and revise. We'll use "issues" in this repository to define your own personal goals, self-evaluate, and revise over the semester. It'll be a living document.

Your goals should be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, and Timely.
You should have between 3 and 5 goals at any time, some near-term and some longer-term.
I ask that goals be **peer-approved**.
Within two days of creating a new goal (as an issue in the repository), please discuss the goal statement with a student in class and have them confirm that the goal meets the SMART criteria. After that discussion, make a comment in the issue "Approved-by: Friendly Neighbor", and note any feedback. (You can also edit the details of the goal as a result of this feedback.) If you prefer to talk with me about a goal, feel free to catch me after class or pick another time to meet with me.

### Specific

We're here to become better learners, and that requires making our abstract or longer-term goals concrete. Behaviors such as starting activities early, discussing with peers or asking for help, and applying concepts in real-world applications are examples of specific behaviors.

### Measurable

Your goal should be objectively measurable. If you share the statement of your goal a log of what you've done with a peer, they should reach the same conclusion of whether the goal has been achieved. It should be possible to fail (your goals are too easy if you never fail).

### Attainable

Goals can be ambitious, but should be attainable if you apply yourself and don't encounter unforeseen obstacles. We'll revise and recalibrate goals as we go through the semester.

### Relevant

This class does not exist in a vacuum, and achievement of your goals in numerical computation may depend on other aspects of your life. I encourage you to set and reflect on goals in other realms of your life, but please write goals here that are relevant to this class.

### Timely

Goals should have a timeline on which they can be assessed. Goals with a timeline from a week to a month are good because you'll be able to assess whether they were completed and set new goals, versus a difficult-to-assess sense of progress during the entire term.

## Example goals

> I will prepare a notebook demo (like a figure) of a concept from class and one formative assessment question each week. I'll present this in a 5-minute demo each Saturday when I talk with my parents and record how it is received and how they answer the question in my journal.

> Each week, I will find a Python package (e.g., using [GitHub Codesearch](https://github.com/search)) that implements an algorithm discussed in class using Julia. I will run a test of accuracy as the condition number of the problem increases or of efficiency as the problem size increases, and share results with a figure on Zulip.

> Humor helps me remember things. Each week, I'll talk with my friend in class to identify three important points and turn one of them into a meme (using [imgflip](https://imgflip.com/memegenerator) or similar) that we'll post to Zulip. I'll record the runners-up in my journal.

## [Journal](journal.md)

It's important to regularly reflect on your progress and strategy.
[Failure is a healthy and normal part of learning](https://www.lifescied.org/doi/10.1187/cbe.18-06-0108): it's how we adapt and cope that matters. 
![](https://www.lifescied.org/cms/10.1187/cbe.18-06-0108/asset/images/large/cbe-18-ar11-g004.jpeg)

You are asked to write journal entries at least once per week. The journal entries should reflect on how things are going and relate your thinking and actions to your active goals. These entries should be *intentional*, but should rarely take more than 5-10 minutes per week.


## Portfolio

I ask that you occasionally (at least twice over the course of the semester) chase a thread of interest beyond the scope or depth covered in class. This could be investigating how a concept from class manifests in another domain, or just exploring more advanced attributes of a concept from class. In doing so, you should create some artifacts that reflect your understanding and put those in the repository. There is no restriction on the form such artifacts can take; common examples might include a markdown write-up, a notebook, code, data created via a comparative study, and/or a short presentation. Please strive to conduct your explorations using [principles and methods of reproducible and ethical research](https://book.the-turing-way.org/).
